# MediQuery — Strategic Acquisition Brief

**Acquisition candidate:** MediQuery v1.0.0  
**Release:** `release/v1.0.0`  
**Repository:** `Akumo961/MediQuery`

## Executive summary

MediQuery is a healthcare-oriented medical-report software foundation focused on secure report ingestion, authentication, owner-scoped access, conservative structured extraction, evidence preservation, report lifecycle management, testing, and deployment documentation.

The acquisition thesis is **speed, reusable engineering foundation, and technical transition**. It is not a claim that the current release is a clinically validated medical-AI product. A buyer can extend the foundation with its own AI, clinical workflows, compliance program, infrastructure, integrations, and distribution.

## What the buyer receives

Subject to definitive agreements and due diligence, the proposed asset package includes:

- source code and repository history;
- application architecture and technical documentation;
- authentication and authorization foundation;
- owner/tenant-scoped report access patterns;
- PDF validation and upload controls;
- report and finding data model;
- conservative evidence-preserving extraction workflow;
- audit and operational logging patterns;
- usage and entitlement foundations;
- automated tests and CI quality gates;
- deployment configuration/documentation;
- security and AI-safety documentation;
- acquisition data-room materials; and
- separately agreed technical knowledge transfer.

The definitive purchase agreement must define the exact transferred assets, rights, warranties, and transition obligations.

## Buyer value

### 1. Reusable foundation

The buyer receives an existing account/report lifecycle, validation, extraction, authorization, testing, and operational foundation rather than starting from an empty repository.

### 2. Evidence-first design

Extracted findings are designed to retain source evidence and page context. This creates a useful foundation for future AI-assisted workflows where outputs need to remain traceable to source material.

### 3. Healthcare-aware engineering

The release includes authentication, owner-scoped access, defensive PDF validation, generated storage keys, security-oriented configuration, request tracing, and PHI-conscious logging patterns.

These controls are engineering foundations only and do **not** constitute regulatory certification or legal compliance.

### 4. Extension path

A buyer can add its own production AI/LLM stack, retrieval, clinical workflows, integrations, compliance controls, polished frontend/mobile experiences, monitoring, and distribution without treating the current repository as a finished clinical product.

## Current capability boundary

The acquisition package must be presented accurately:

- no clinical validation is claimed;
- no diagnostic or medical-advice capability should be claimed;
- production generative-AI capability must not be assumed merely from architecture or documentation;
- production PHI authorization is not established by this repository alone;
- HIPAA, PIPEDA, PHIPA, GDPR, SOC 2, FDA, Health Canada, or equivalent certification/approval is not claimed;
- customers and revenue must be independently evidenced before being represented;
- private/ignored models, datasets, or assets must be identified and transferred separately when applicable; and
- third-party licenses and IP ownership remain subject to formal diligence.

## Why acquire instead of build?

The strongest acquisition argument is not that every component is technically unique. A competent buyer could reproduce many individual components. The argument is that the buyer can obtain a coherent healthcare-oriented foundation, tests, documentation, security patterns, and technical context while saving time and avoiding early architectural decisions.

The acquisition is most compelling when the buyer:

1. has an active medical-document or patient-report roadmap;
2. values rapid time-to-prototype or pilot;
3. can reuse its existing AI, cloud, identity, and compliance infrastructure;
4. needs a healthcare-oriented report workflow; and
5. values technical knowledge transfer from the builder.

## Ideal buyer profile

Priority targets:

- Canadian and North American digital-health startups;
- healthcare SaaS companies adding medical-document workflows;
- laboratory or diagnostic organizations developing patient-facing report experiences;
- patient-navigation and care-coordination platforms;
- healthcare software agencies seeking a reusable or white-label foundation; and
- smaller strategic acquirers with limited internal bandwidth.

Large enterprises may have strategic fit, but they will normally impose higher diligence requirements and a stronger build-vs-buy comparison.

## Positioning

> **MediQuery is a privacy-first, evidence-preserving medical-document software foundation for turning laboratory reports into structured, auditable information through a secure, tenant-isolated architecture.**

## 30-second acquisition pitch

> We built MediQuery as a healthcare-oriented document-intelligence foundation rather than a consumer medical chatbot. The current release provides secure report ingestion, authentication, owner isolation, conservative structured extraction, evidence preservation, audit-oriented workflows, tests, CI, and deployment documentation. The acquisition value is speed: a strategic buyer can acquire a coherent foundation and extend it with its existing AI, clinical, compliance, and distribution capabilities instead of rebuilding the underlying platform from zero.

## Technical buyer pitch

> The strongest technical assets are the account/report lifecycle, owner-scoped authorization pattern, defensive PDF validation, evidence-linked finding schema, conservative extraction workflow, security-oriented configuration, automated tests, and documentation. The repository is explicit about its limitations, allowing an engineering team to assess the asset without relying on unsupported marketing claims.

## Business buyer pitch

> MediQuery provides a shortcut to a healthcare-document product foundation. The buyer can acquire an existing technical base and focus its internal resources on the differentiated layers that matter most to its business: AI, clinical workflows, integrations, compliance, UX, and distribution.

## $100K+ positioning

A $100K+ transaction should **not** be justified by claiming that the current source code alone has a $100K replacement cost. The defensible framing is a combination of:

- reusable technical assets;
- time-to-market acceleration;
- documented architecture and tests;
- reduced initial implementation risk;
- strategic fit with a buyer's roadmap; and
- optional founder-led technical transition, if included in the deal.

The final price is a negotiated commercial outcome, not an independent valuation.

## Commercial posture

Do not publicly guarantee a valuation. Qualify buyer interest first. If strategic interest is demonstrated, an asking-price anchor can be negotiated based on:

- IP scope;
- exclusivity;
- transition/support period;
- buyer's intended use;
- diligence burden;
- strategic urgency; and
- competing interest.

Keep multiple qualified conversations active where practical so the seller is not negotiating from a single-buyer position.

## Acquisition process

1. Confidential targeted outreach
2. Mutual NDA where appropriate
3. Executive/product discussion
4. Technical demonstration
5. Controlled Buyer Data Room access
6. Technical, security, and IP diligence
7. Commercial discussion
8. Letter of Intent, if appropriate
9. Definitive purchase agreement
10. IP/asset transfer and payment
11. Agreed technical transition

## Diligence package

The buyer should be able to verify:

- exact release and commit identity;
- complete source repository;
- dependency and license inventory;
- IP ownership and contributor representations;
- security/test/CI evidence;
- deployment documentation;
- known limitations;
- third-party service dependencies;
- domain/brand/assets if included; and
- any private models, datasets, or ignored assets required for operation.

## Claims policy

Never represent MediQuery as clinically validated, a diagnostic device, an AI doctor, regulatory-certified, FDA/Health Canada approved, authorized for real-PHI production use, revenue-generating, enterprise-adopted, patented, or guaranteed to have a particular monetary value unless independent evidence supports the specific statement.

## Central buyer question

> **Does acquiring this existing healthcare-oriented foundation, together with its documentation and optional technical transition, create enough time-to-market or strategic value to justify purchasing it rather than reproducing the foundation internally?**

That is the core acquisition thesis.
