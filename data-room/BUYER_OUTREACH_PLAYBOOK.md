# MediQuery — Buyer Outreach Playbook

## Purpose

Convert the acquisition package into a disciplined, confidential buyer-outreach process.

This playbook is designed to maximize the probability of finding a qualified strategic buyer without overstating MediQuery's current capabilities.

## Target buyer tiers

### Tier 1 — Highest priority

Target founders, CEOs, CTOs, Heads of Product, or Heads of Engineering at:

- Canadian digital-health startups;
- healthcare SaaS companies;
- patient-navigation/care-coordination platforms;
- laboratory-report or diagnostic software companies; and
- healthcare software agencies building reusable platforms.

**Reason:** these organizations can have a direct product need while still benefiting materially from an existing foundation and technical transition.

### Tier 2 — Strategic healthcare organizations

Approach product/innovation/technology leaders at:

- laboratory organizations;
- virtual-care companies;
- health-benefits technology businesses;
- clinical workflow software vendors; and
- larger health-tech organizations.

Expect greater diligence and longer procurement/acquisition cycles.

### Tier 3 — Large enterprises

Large organizations such as major Canadian healthcare/technology companies may be contacted as strategic prospects, but do not assume that company size implies acquisition interest. The pitch must focus on a concrete roadmap problem and time-to-market benefit.

## Buyer qualification checklist

Before spending significant diligence time, establish:

- Does the buyer have a medical-document/report workflow problem?
- Is there an active roadmap where MediQuery could accelerate delivery?
- Does the buyer have its own AI/cloud/compliance infrastructure?
- Can the buyer make a software/IP acquisition?
- Is there an executive sponsor with authority to evaluate the asset?
- Is a founder/engineer transition useful to the buyer?
- Does the buyer understand that the current release is a foundation rather than a clinically validated AI product?

Prioritize buyers who answer yes to several of these questions.

## Outreach sequence

### Stage 1 — Short confidential message

Do not send the entire repository or valuation thesis in the first contact.

Objective: obtain a 15–20 minute conversation with a qualified decision-maker.

Suggested subject:

**Healthcare software acquisition opportunity — MediQuery**

Suggested message:

> I built MediQuery, a healthcare-oriented medical-report software foundation focused on secure report ingestion, authenticated access, owner isolation, evidence-preserving extraction, testing, and documented deployment/security patterns. I'm exploring a strategic sale to a health-tech company that could reuse the foundation and extend it with its existing AI and clinical infrastructure. If this is relevant to your roadmap, I'd be happy to share a short acquisition brief and technical demo under appropriate confidentiality.

Do not claim clinical validation, regulatory certification, production AI, customers, revenue, or a guaranteed valuation.

### Stage 2 — Qualification call

Ask about the buyer's roadmap before giving a detailed valuation discussion.

Questions:

1. Are medical reports/documents currently part of your product roadmap?
2. What portion of the workflow is currently built internally?
3. Is time-to-market a current constraint?
4. Would an existing backend/report foundation be useful?
5. Do you already operate an AI/LLM stack?
6. What security/compliance requirements would the asset need to satisfy?
7. Would technical transition from the builder have value?

### Stage 3 — NDA and controlled diligence

If strategic interest is real:

- execute an NDA when appropriate;
- provide the Buyer Data Room;
- identify the exact release/commit;
- provide the demo runbook and evidence;
- answer technical questions using repository evidence;
- disclose limitations early.

Never hide known limitations to preserve negotiating leverage. Credibility is part of the asset.

### Stage 4 — Technical/product demo

Use the `DEMO_RUNBOOK.md` process. Demonstrate the actual release, not a mockup or future roadmap.

The demo should emphasize:

- secure account/report lifecycle;
- validation;
- extraction;
- evidence/page traceability;
- access isolation;
- deletion;
- tests/CI;
- architecture;
- documented extension path.

### Stage 5 — Commercial discussion

Discuss price only after the buyer understands the asset and identifies a strategic use case.

Frame the price around:

- transferred IP/assets;
- time saved;
- speed to a buyer-specific prototype/pilot;
- technical documentation;
- optional transition/support;
- strategic urgency; and
- transaction scope.

Do not present an unsupported valuation as an objective market fact.

## Negotiation strategy

Maintain multiple qualified conversations where possible.

Do not reveal the identity or confidential details of another prospect without permission.

Avoid artificial bidding claims. Competitive tension must be real.

If a buyer asks, "Why don't we just build this?", answer directly:

> You can. The question is whether acquiring the existing foundation and technical context is cheaper in time, opportunity cost, and execution risk than starting from zero for your particular roadmap.

If a buyer asks, "Why $100K+?":

> The price is based on the strategic value of acquiring an existing healthcare-oriented foundation, the time-to-market it can save, the documented engineering work already completed, and the optional technical transition. It is not based on claiming that every line of the current code would cost $100K to reproduce.

## Materials by stage

### First contact

- one-line positioning;
- short confidential message.

### After interest

- Strategic Acquisition Brief;
- high-level architecture;
- demo invitation.

### After NDA

- Buyer Data Room;
- source/diligence evidence;
- security/IP documentation;
- demo evidence.

### Serious buyer

- detailed technical Q&A;
- dependency/license review;
- IP ownership documentation;
- transition plan;
- commercial terms.

## Red flags

Pause or decline the process if a prospect:

- requests real patient data for an informal demo;
- asks the seller to make unsupported compliance/clinical claims;
- refuses reasonable confidentiality for non-public materials;
- requests transfer of credentials or secrets before a transaction framework exists;
- demands broad exclusivity before serious commercial commitment; or
- asks for material engineering work without a clear acquisition/pilot agreement.

## Success criteria

Outreach is successful when it produces qualified conversations with buyers that have a concrete product reason to acquire the asset.

The objective is **not** maximum number of messages. The objective is a small number of credible buyers who can evaluate MediQuery on strategic value.
