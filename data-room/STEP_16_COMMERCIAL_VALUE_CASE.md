# Step 16 — Commercial Value Case

## Objective

Give buyers a rational framework for discussing a $100K+ acquisition without claiming that the repository itself has a guaranteed market price.

## Value drivers

### 1. Time saved

The buyer receives an existing healthcare-oriented backend foundation rather than beginning the initial architecture and implementation from zero.

### 2. Risk reduction

The buyer can inspect an existing implementation, tests, documentation, and security patterns before deciding what to retain or replace.

### 3. Technical transition

The builder can potentially provide context and transition support, subject to a negotiated agreement.

### 4. Strategic acceleration

The asset may be valuable when it directly accelerates a buyer's active roadmap for medical documents, patient workflows, or evidence-oriented data extraction.

### 5. Option value

A buyer may acquire the foundation and extend it with its own AI, clinical, infrastructure, compliance, and distribution capabilities.

## Price framing

A $100K+ transaction should be justified by the combination of IP/assets, time-to-market acceleration, strategic fit, and transition value. It should not be justified by claiming that the current code would necessarily cost $100K to reproduce.

## Negotiation anchor

A seller may open above the desired minimum to leave room for negotiation, but the final number should follow buyer interest, transaction scope, and diligence findings.

## Evidence required for a premium

The strongest factors for moving above $100K are:

- demonstrated buyer-specific use case;
- real technical demo;
- reproducible tests;
- clean IP ownership;
- strong security review;
- real user/customer evidence if available;
- live deployment evidence if available;
- working differentiated AI capability if later added; and
- meaningful founder transition/support.

## Exit criterion

A buyer can understand exactly what the requested price represents and what evidence would support a higher strategic premium.
